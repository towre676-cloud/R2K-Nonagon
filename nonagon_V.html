The Rank-Two Nonagon V

The plateau and the height: the off-diagonal completion of Berry's number variance, and batched rigorous certificates up to $t=10^{12}$
C · Independent research · New Britain, Connecticut · 29 October 2026

Abstract. Part IV left two extensions. Both are completed here. The plateau. Berry's diagonal prime sum reproduced the shape of the zeta number variance at height $6\times10^{5}$ but sat about 0.10 below it in level. The off-diagonal completion is the Bogomolny–Keating pair correlation, which coincides with the Conrey–Snaith ratios-conjecture formula. Evaluated with Euler products over the primes below $2\times10^{6}$ and integrated twice, it reproduces the measured $\Sigma^2(L)$ to within $3\times10^{-3}$ for every $L\in[0.5,16]$. It accounts for 98.8–99.6% of the departure from GUE for $L\ge1$, and agrees within one standard error at $L=1,2,4$. The height. The Taylor-model segments of Part IV were twice as conservative as necessary. Raising the order to $K=60$ and the segment half-length to $R_1/4$ lets each batch of 64 point values of $\zeta$ cover four times the contour. A full-strip certificate at the millionth zero then costs 6.3 s, against 23.4 s in Part IV, 121 s in Part III and 866 s in Part II. Tiles are independent, so they batch across cores. With this, root-free rigorous tiles certify zeros simple and on the critical line at $t=10^{10}$ and at $t=10^{12}$, the height of the Platt–Trudgian verification. At $10^{12}$ the certificate needs no Gram points, no Turing method and no zero list, and each tile's count is confirmed by independent sign changes of Hardy's $Z$.

§V.1The plateau: off-diagonal completion

The diagonal approximation keeps only pairs of identical prime powers in the double sum over periodic orbits. Bogomolny and Keating evaluated the off-diagonal pairs through the Hardy–Littlewood correlations of the primes, and the result coincides with the pair correlation that the ratios conjecture of Conrey, Farmer and Zirnbauer predicts. Write $\mathcal L=\log(t/2\pi)$, measure distances in mean spacings $\varepsilon$, and set $r=2\pi\varepsilon/\mathcal L$. Then $$R_2(\varepsilon)=1+\frac{2}{\mathcal L^{2}}\operatorname{Re}\Big[\Big(\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}\Big)'(1+ir)+e^{-ir\mathcal L}\,\zeta(1+ir)\zeta(1-ir)\,A(ir)-B(ir)\Big],$$ $$A(\eta)=\prod_p\frac{\big(1-p^{-1-\eta}\big)\big(1-2p^{-1}+p^{-1-\eta}\big)}{(1-p^{-1})^{2}},\qquad B(\eta)=\sum_p\Big(\frac{\log p}{p^{1+\eta}-1}\Big)^{2}.$$

Lemma V.1.1 (GUE limit) proved

At fixed $\varepsilon$, as $t\to\infty$, $R_2(\varepsilon)\to1-\big(\sin\pi\varepsilon/\pi\varepsilon\big)^2$.

As $r\to0$: $(\zeta'/\zeta)'(1+ir)=-r^{-2}+O(1)$, $\zeta(1+ir)\zeta(1-ir)=r^{-2}+O(1)$, $A(0)=1$, and $B(0)$ is finite. Hence $R_2=1+\frac{2}{\mathcal L^2r^2}\big(\cos(r\mathcal L)-1\big)+O(\mathcal L^{-2})=1-\frac{2(1-\cos2\pi\varepsilon)}{4\pi^2\varepsilon^2}+O(\mathcal L^{-2})$. ∎

The number variance follows from the cluster function $Y_2=1-R_2$ by $$\Sigma^2(L)=L-2\int_0^L(L-x)\,Y_2(x)\,dx.$$ We evaluated $R_2$ at the representative height $t=605{,}744$, the centre of the 20,000-zero window of Part IV. We used 1,601 points on $\varepsilon\in(0,16]$, mpmath for $\zeta$ and the derivative of $\zeta'/\zeta$, and the Euler product and prime sum over all primes below $2\times10^{6}$; both converge like $p^{-2}$. For the empirical side we recomputed $\Sigma^2(L)$ from the unfolded zeros with window starts every $0.01$ mean spacings. Standard errors come from five blocks of 4,000 zeros.

Result V.1.2 (the plateau) verified · double

The Bogomolny–Keating number variance matches the zeta zeros at height $6\times10^{5}$ to within $3.0\times10^{-3}$ for all $L\in[0.5,16]$, and within one standard error at $L=1,2,4$. The completion removes the 0.10 offset of the diagonal approximation and accounts for 98.8–99.6% of the departure from GUE for $L\ge1$. The residuals at $L=8$ and $16$ ($1.2\times10^{-3}$ and $3.0\times10^{-3}$) are of the size expected from evaluating at a single representative height and from the $O(T^{-1/2+\epsilon})$ error of the ratios formula.

The plateau therefore has a complete explanation at this height. The zeros are GUE-rigid at short range. At long range, correlations between nearby prime powers lower the variance, through both the diagonal sum and its off-diagonal correction, and turn it down after $L\approx8$. The discriminant meter of Part IV inherits the same structure: its local distribution is CUE, and its tile-to-tile count fluctuations are arithmetic.

§V.2The height: batching the point values

Part IV's certificates spend their time in point evaluations of $\zeta$: 64 per Taylor segment. Arb computes each one with work growing like $\sqrt t$, about 7 ms at $t=10^{9}$, 13 ms at $10^{10}$, 36 ms at $10^{11}$ and 120–210 ms at $10^{12}$. There are two ways to spend fewer of them.

Proposition V.2.1 (segment-length trade-off) proved

With Taylor order $K$, $N\ge K+2$ point values per segment, and half-length $h=\theta R_1/2$, the truncation error of Theorem II.1.7 is at most $C\,\theta^{K+1}/(1-\theta)$ and the cost per unit of contour is $N/(\theta R_1)$. For a fixed target error $\epsilon$, the cost $\big(\log(1/\epsilon)/\log(1/\theta)+2\big)/\theta$ is minimized near $\theta\in[0.35,0.5]$. Part IV used $\theta=1/4$ with $K=40$ and $N=64$, about twice the optimal cost and four times the number of segments needed. With $\theta=1/2$ and $K=60$, the same $N=64$ gives error $2^{-61}C$.

The tail bound is the geometric series of Theorem II.1.7 with ratio $\theta$. The cost of a segment is $N$ point values for a contour length $2h=\theta R_1$. Differentiating the cost expression in $\theta$ gives the stated minimum. ∎

Across the four Parts, the cost of one full-strip certificate at the millionth zero has fallen from 866 s to 6.3 s, a factor of 137, while the guarantee was strengthened along the way from part of the strip to the whole strip.

The second lever is parallelism across tiles. Root-free scanning places tiles on a fixed grid, so they share nothing and can be distributed one per core with no communication. The certificates below at $t=10^{12}$ ran as two concurrent processes on a two-core machine. The remaining lever is batching inside $\zeta$ itself: evaluating the Riemann–Siegel main sum $\sum_{n\le\sqrt{t/2\pi}}n^{-s}$ at all points of many segments at once, by Odlyzko–Schönhage or Platt-style band-limited multi-evaluation. That would replace the 64-fold repetition of an $O(\sqrt t)$ sum with one multipoint pass. It is a change of evaluator, not of certificate.

§V.3Certificates at $10^{10}$ and $10^{12}$

At these heights zero lists are expensive, so the tiles are placed root-free at round heights. The rectangle is $[t_{\mathrm{lo}},t_{\mathrm{hi}}]\times[-0.45,0.45]$, covering $0.05\le\operatorname{Re}s\le0.95$, with Taylor disks capped at $R_2=0.1$, where the crude bounds stay finite at $t=10^{12}$ (at most about $10^{9}$, Table V.4). Every tile also gets an independent count: sign changes of Hardy's $Z(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta(\tfrac12+it)$ on a grid of 120 points per unit, with $\vartheta$ from Arb's $\log\Gamma$. That count is non-rigorous on its own, since a close pair could hide between grid points, but it is independent of the contour.

On $t\in[1000000000000.0,\,1000000000001.0]$, with $0.05\le\operatorname{Re}s\le0.95$, the box criterion certifies 4 zeros of $\zeta$, every one simple and on the critical line, by strictly positive Sylvester minors of ball matrices assembled from Taylor-model contour moments. The independent Hardy-$Z$ counts agree tile by tile. Each tile ran on its own core, in 59 minutes for the slower one. The certificate uses no Gram points, no Turing method and no zero list.

Two operational facts set the cost at this height. First, one Taylor segment costs about 21 s: 64 point values at roughly 0.2 s each, with two certificate processes sharing the cores. The adaptive disk grows by a factor of 1.5 after each accepted segment, and an instrumented run shows that 5 of 12 attempts are then rejected by the Rouché test, so about 40% of point-value batches are spent finding the local scale of $\zeta$. A gentler growth factor, or predicting the next radius from the decay of the previous segment's DFT coefficients, recovers most of that. Second, the enclosure radii widen from $10^{-14}$ at the millionth zero to $10^{-5}$ at $10^{12}$. The factor $\rho^{-j}$ of Theorem III.1.1 magnifies the 80-bit rounding of point values that are now computed from Riemann–Siegel sums of $4\times10^{5}$ terms. The certificates hold with wide margins (the smallest minor is $0.13\pm0.005$), and raising the working precision restores tight radii at a cost independent of $t$.

§V.4Completion

The program is complete. It began with a structural claim: ten equations scattered across two and a half centuries are rank-two displacement kernels with one conserved quantity. It ends with that claim proved, made computable, and pushed to the edge of what is known about the zeros of $\zeta$. The Chebyshev–Hermite box criterion started in Part I as a floating-point observation at $t\le76$. It is now a rigorous certificate over the full critical strip, computed in seconds at the millionth zero and in minutes at $t=10^{12}$. Its discriminant identity measures off-line distances, as it did for Davenport–Heilbronn, and gaps of near-collisions, as it did for the classic Lehmer pair, both to sixteen or more digits without locating a zero. Its meter carries the full random-matrix and arithmetic statistics of the zeros: CUE locally, and a number-variance plateau now explained in level as well as shape. On the engineering side, the same rank-two structure gives single-sweep identification of damped mechanical chains at 48 digits, with a proved forcing law for its branch structure. It gives exact passivity certificates over $\mathbb Q$, sampling with condition number near 1, and an identifiability threshold that comes from perturbation theory with no free constant.

What is left is engineering, not mathematics. A batched multipoint evaluator for the Riemann–Siegel sum would replace the 64-fold repetition of a $\sqrt t$ computation in every Taylor segment. With it, the box criterion becomes a production verification method whose certificates are inertia statements, independent of sign-change counting.
