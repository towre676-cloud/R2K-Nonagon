Abstract. The v10.1 release reconciled two independent Part X branches. This addendum makes the four adjustments that the reconciliation required of the Claude branch, and runs each one before stating it. Passivity erratum. Theorems VI.4.1 and X.5.1 accepted $\operatorname{Re}Z\ge0$ on the axis only when the axis polynomial $P$ has no positive root. Positive-real functions may touch zero, giving positive roots of even multiplicity. Both certificates are therefore sound but incomplete, and their "if and only if" is withdrawn. The minimal example is $Z=(s^2+1)/(s^2+s+1)$, which both reject and which is positive real. Against the v10.1 Cayley/Brune checker, the Claude certificate never accepts a non-passive function, and every disagreement is of this one kind. The Part VI ladder decisions are unchanged. The v10.1 checker becomes the pipeline default. Edge law. Against the v10.1 nuisance-compensated far-endpoint floor, the protocol's edge errors on the 9-node path sit at a constant 62–66 times the efficient value for every interior edge. The far endpoint is confirmed as the governing quantity, and the exponent gap of Result X.4.2 is closed. Composition and locality. Composing $k$ tiles of the Part III scan with the v10.1 root-polynomial basis at 192 bits decides the parent inertia up to 10 zeros, against 5 for the flat power-basis form, and fails from 12 zeros on because the basis determinant becomes undecidable. Composition obeys the locality law rather than escaping it. Large ranges are certified, completely and without any parent form, as the conjunction of tile certificates.

§X·2.1Passivity: from sound to complete

Let $Z=N/D$ with $N,D$ coprime over $\mathbb Q$, and let $P(x)=\operatorname{Re}[N(i\omega)D(-i\omega)]$ at $x=\omega^2$. Positive-realness needs $P\ge0$ on $[0,\infty)$. That holds exactly when, after the zero root is removed, $P$ is positive just to the right of 0 and has no positive root of odd multiplicity. Theorem VI.4.1 (and through it Theorem X.5.1) instead required no positive root at all, because the Hermite count of §VI.4 counts distinct roots and ignores multiplicity. The consequence is one-sided. Any function they accept is positive real. A positive-real function whose real part touches zero on the axis, a lossless tangency, is rejected.

Erratum X·2.1.1 (to Theorems VI.4.1 and X.5.1) proved

In both theorems, replace "$P$ has no positive root" by "$P$, divided by its zero-root power, has positive lowest coefficient and no positive root of odd multiplicity", decided by square-free factorization and Sturm counts over $\mathbb Q$. With this change, Theorem X.5.1 is the exact characterization: $N+D$ strictly Hurwitz and the corrected axis condition. As originally stated, both theorems are sufficient conditions only.

§X·2.2The edge law against the compensated floor

Result X.4.2 found that the protocol resolves a path edge at a rate set by the farther endpoint, while its provable floor used the nearer one. The v10.1 diagonal-congruence construction supplies the missing floor. Scaling node $v$ by $t=1+\delta$ leaves the baseline port data invariant. Calibrated grounds break the invariance by at most $S\delta/(2+\delta)$, where $S=G_{0v}^2/G_{vv}$. This gives an efficient standard deviation $2\sigma wG_{vv}/(\sqrt m\,G_{0v}^2)$ along the gauge for any protocol with $m$ measurements.

For every interior edge from $(1,2)$ to $(6,7)$, the protocol's error is $62$–$66$ times the compensated efficient value. The far-endpoint compliance $G_{0v}$ is therefore the governing quantity in both the achievable rate and the floor, with identical exponent $-2$. The remaining factor is a property of the explicit protocol, which spends a fixed share of its measurements on any one edge, and not of the information available. A protocol that concentrates measurements on the far endpoint at $\theta G_{vv}=1$, attains the efficient value within the one-parameter diagonal-congruence family: the v10.1 estimation example, 80,000 replicates, gives an empirical-to-efficient ratio of 1.00004.

§X·2.3Composition and the locality law

The v10.1 nested-box construction composes two adjacent certified tiles. It forms the parent moment form from the children's moments, mapped into parent coordinates, and transforms it by a basis whose columns are child root polynomials times powers. The cross blocks vanish exactly, so the parent inertia is the sum of the children's. Observation X.2.1 says that no single moment form can be decided once a box holds more than about 19 zeros. The question is whether composition escapes that limit or meets it. The test composes $k$ consecutive tiles of the Part III scan over $[6990,6990+2k]$, up to $k=15$ (34 zeros). It regenerates each tile at 192 bits with moments up to order $2N-2=66$ and applies the v10.1 construction unchanged at every $k$.

At 192 bits, the flat parent form in the power basis is decided up to 5 zeros (2 tiles) and no further. Composition decides the parent inertia up to 10 zeros (4 tiles), correctly $(N,0)$ in every case. From 12 zeros on, the sign of $\det V$ itself is undecided. The basis of child root polynomials is a resultant-type matrix, and its determinant shrinks as tiles are added. The cross blocks, exactly zero in exact arithmetic, grow in enclosure from $10^{-14}$ at two tiles to $10^{-4}$ at fifteen.

Composition therefore does not escape the locality law. It moves the limit for the power-basis form from 5 to 10 zeros at 192 bits, and then meets the same Chebyshev–Vandermonde conditioning in the transformation matrix. What it contributes is a consistency theorem: the parent moments overlap the sum of the child moments, and for small unions the parent inertia is the sum of the child inertias. For certifying large ranges, the conjunction of child certificates is already the complete proof. The zeros of a union of disjoint closed boxes, each with a zero-free certified boundary, are the union of their zeros, and reality and simplicity are local properties. No parent form is needed, and at scale none can be decided. The two Part X branches agree on this once it is stated: v10.1 composes small unions algebraically, and the locality law explains why large unions are certified tile by tile.

§X·2.4Ledger after reconciliation

The Claude branch's Part X now stands as follows. Theorem X.3.1 is superseded by the v10.1 all-dimension statement, which uses the uniform arrowhead witness. Theorem X.5.1 is corrected by Erratum X·2.1.1 and superseded in practice by the v10.1 checker. Result X.4.2 is completed by the compensated floor, up to a measured protocol constant. Theorem X.0.1 remains an independent spot confirmation of the uniform v10.1 origin certificate, with which it agrees. Observation X.2.1 and its relation to composition are as stated in §X·2.3. The rejections of Haynsworth pivots and Dodgson condensation as precision devices stand. The v10.1 basis policy uses condensation as an operation-count device with a guarded fallback, which is a different and compatible role.
