Taylor-model integration: removing the wrapping barrier

At height, Arb's adaptive integrator stalls. Point evaluations of $\xi'/\xi$ at $t=7005$ take about 5 ms and return radius $10^{-18}$. A ball input of radius $10^{-3}$, however, returns a non-finite enclosure, and at radius $10^{-4}$ the output radius is $3.5$. The amplification factor, output radius divided by input radius, grows from about $3\times10^{2}$ at $t=70$ to $8\times10^{3}$ at $t=1419$ and $2.6\times10^{4}$ at $t=7005$. This is the wrapping effect of the Euler–Maclaurin derivative bounds Arb uses to propagate input radii, and it is an overestimate: the true local variation of $\zeta'/\zeta$ at distance 0.45 from the zeros is of order 1. Gauss–Legendre error bounds need the integrand enclosed on whole Bernstein ellipses, so the integrator subdivides almost without end. The tile around the classic Lehmer pair ran for over 100 minutes at 80 bits, and a 64-bit attempt for 60 minutes, neither finishing.

Theorem II.1.7 (Taylor-model segment enclosure) proved

Let a contour segment have centre $t_c$ and half-length $h$ in the local variable $w = t - t_c$. Let $z_j$ be the Taylor coefficients of $Z(w)=\zeta(\tfrac12+i(t_c+w))$ to order $K+1$, computed in ball arithmetic at the exact point $t_c$. Let $M_2\ge\sup_{|w|\le R_2}|Z|$ be a finite crude ball bound, and set $R_1=R_2/2$. If $$m_1 := |z_0| - \sum_{1\le j\le K+1}|z_j|R_1^{\,j} - M_2\,2^{-(K+1)}\ \gt\ 0,$$ then $Z$ has no zero in $|w|\le R_1$, and $|Z'/Z|\le 2M_1/(R_1m_1)$ on $|w|\le R_1/2$, where $M_1$ is the corresponding upper bound. If moreover $h\le R_1/8$, then $$\int_{t_c-h}^{t_c+h}T_k(u(t))\,\tfrac{d}{dt}\log\Xi(t)\,dt = \sum_{j\le K,\ j\ \mathrm{even}}P_j\,\frac{2h^{j+1}}{j+1}\ \pm\ 2h\,M_fM_T\,\frac{4^{-(K+1)}}{1-1/4}.$$ Here the $P_j$ are the exact-point Taylor coefficients of $T_k(u)\cdot\frac{d}{dw}\log\xi$, and $M_f$, $M_T$ bound $|\frac{d}{dw}\log\xi|$ and $|T_k(u)|$ on $|w|\le R_1/2$.

On $|w|\le R_1$, Cauchy's estimate on the disk of radius $R_2$ bounds the tail of $Z$ beyond order $K+1$ by $M_2\sum_{j\ge K+2}2^{-j} = M_2 2^{-(K+1)}$, and the triangle inequality then gives $|Z|\ge m_1$. On $|w|\le R_1/2$, Cauchy's estimate on the radius-$R_1$ disk bounds $|Z'|$ by $M_1/(R_1/2)$. The gamma-factor part $\tfrac1s+\tfrac1{s-1}-\tfrac12\log\pi+\tfrac12\psi(s/2)$ has a tight ball enclosure at height and is added directly. The integrand's Taylor series converges on $|w|\le R_1/2$, and on $|w|\le h\le R_1/8$ its tail beyond order $K$ is at most $M_fM_T\,4^{-(K+1)}/(1-\tfrac14)$. The polynomial part integrates exactly, and odd powers cancel on a symmetric segment. Segment endpoints are exact dyadics shared by neighbouring segments, so the pieces tile each side exactly. ∎

The decisive point is where crude bounds enter. The crude bound $M_2$ appears only multiplied by $2^{-(K+1)}$, and the crude Cauchy bound $M_f$ only by $4^{-(K+1)}$. With $K=40$, a bound overestimated by $10^{6}$ costs nothing. Ball evaluation is needed only on disks small enough to stay finite, about $R_2\le0.02$ at these heights, and all precision comes from point evaluations, where Arb is tight. The segment size adapts: $R_2$ is halved until the Rouché test passes, and grows back by a factor of 1.5 afterwards.

On the first tile the integrator reproduces Arb's own, $\boldsymbol\mu_0=1\pm6\times10^{-21}$. A first version that accumulated segment positions in floating point failed this validation, with $\mu_0=1.0000000000000149\pm3.6\times10^{-18}$, an enclosure that excludes the true value. The seams between 5,000 segments leaked $10^{-14}$, and exact dyadic boundaries removed the leak.

From $t=1419$ to $t=74{,}921$ the segment count per tile stays between 2,290 and 3,064, essentially independent of height: the wrapping barrier is gone. What remains is the cost of one exact-point Taylor series of $\zeta$, which Arb computes by Euler–Maclaurin with work growing linearly in $t$: 15 s per tile at the Lehmer height, 133 s at height 74,921. This residual is problem N1 in its sharpened form.
