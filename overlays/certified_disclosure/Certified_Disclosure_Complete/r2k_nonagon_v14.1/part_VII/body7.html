§VII.1Three routes over the first ninety units of height

The frontier engine and the R2K engine certify the same object, the Chebyshev–Hermite moments of $\frac{d}{dt}\log\Xi$ on a rectangle, by different numerical mathematics. The frontier engine evaluates $\xi'/\xi$ pointwise in Arb through a length-2 power series, so its derivative is exact at order 1 and the global series cap is irrelevant (the engine also asserts ctx.cap$\,\ge2$). It integrates each side of the full rectangle with acb.integral to a prescribed tolerance, and it certifies a zero-free boundary by a separate ball evaluation on a dyadic partition. The R2K engine expands $\zeta$ to order 60 on disks, integrates term by term with a Cauchy remainder, and uses the conjugation-symmetric half-path. Its proof record is replayed by r2kverify.py, which re-implements every bound. The frontier campaign placed its 45 leaf tiles adaptively on $[10,100]\times[-0.6,0.6]$, two units of height each, and verified its own campaign by a fresh replay.

Result VII.1.1 (tile-by-tile agreement of independent routes) proved · replayed

On each of the 45 frontier tiles, the R2K proof record passes full independent replay. The 45 counts agree with the frontier counts without exception: 18 tiles are empty, 25 contain one zero and 2 contain two, for a total of 29 zeros. Every non-empty tile has inertia $(n,0)$, so every zero of $\zeta$ with $10\le\operatorname{Im}s\le100$ is simple and lies on the critical line. This repeats a fact that has been known for a century. What is new is the agreement of independent certification routes on the same tile boundaries.

§VII.2Damped twins: what one sweep cannot identify

Part II reconstructed a damped mechanical chain from one driving-point response by Cauer-type peeling, and Parts III–IV analyzed the branch structure of that peeling. Every one of those results assumes that the network is a path. The frontier campaign made the assumption's necessity explicit. For unit masses and any orthogonal $Q$ with $Qe_1=e_1$, the systems $K$ and $\widetilde K=Q^{\mathsf T}KQ$ have the same compliance $e_1^{\mathsf T}(K-zI)^{-1}e_1$. With $Q$ a rotation by the Pythagorean angle $\cos\theta=12/13$ in the $(2,3)$-plane, a grounded path with springs $1,1$ and grounds $3,1,1$ becomes a valid positive triangle. The shared compliance is $(z^2-5z+5)/(-z^3+9z^2-24z+18)$.

The same identity holds for the full quadratic pencil. If $M=I$, then $e_1^{\mathsf T}(s^2I+sC+K)^{-1}e_1=e_1^{\mathsf T}(s^2I+s\,Q^{\mathsf T}CQ+Q^{\mathsf T}KQ)^{-1}e_1$ for every $s$, because $Q$ fixes $e_1$. A damped twin therefore exists whenever $Q^{\mathsf T}CQ$ is also a valid damper network. For proportional damping $C=\alpha K$ this is automatic. The question that matters for Part II is whether it can also hold for the non-proportional damping that makes the peeling non-trivial.

Proposition VII.2.1 (a non-proportional damped twin) proved

Take unit masses, the frontier springs $K$ (path: inter-mass $1,1$; grounds $3,1,1$), and path dampers with inter-mass values $d_{12}=17/55$, $d_{23}=161/948$ and ground values $101/472,\;0,\;281/915$. This $C$ is not proportional to $K$. Under the $12/13$ rotation, $Q^{\mathsf T}CQ$ is a valid damper triangle, with inter-mass values $204/715$, $17/143$, $12779849/107502252$ and ground values $40119/337480$, $52413769/268755630$, $31949087/268755630$, all strictly positive. $Q^{\mathsf T}KQ$ is the frontier's positive spring triangle. The damped path and the damped triangle have the same driving-point function $H(s)$ identically. Exact rational evaluation at four points confirms the identity.

The dampers were found by a linear program that maximizes the smallest network margin of the transformed matrix over non-negative path dampers. The maximum is about 0.119, so an open set of non-proportional dampings admits twins under this one rotation. Part II's peeling, applied to this $H$, returns the path, because it is a path-class algorithm, and that is what it should do. The correct statement of Part II is therefore the following. A positive damped path is determined uniquely by one driving-point response within the class of paths, and the class itself must be supplied. The frontier campaign's remedy applies unchanged: additional sensing, or full displacement responses at enough distinct shifts, separates the twins.

§VII.3The number-variance estimator, calibrated

Part VI compared the empirical number variance of 20,000 consecutive zeros with the height-averaged Bogomolny–Keating prediction at three disjoint heights. It found agreement to within one or two block standard errors for $L\le4$, and a deficit of about 0.002–0.003 at $L=8$ and $16$ with the same sign at every height. Two properties of the estimator could produce or distort such a pattern. One is bias: dense sliding windows over a single finite sample, with the mean count estimated from the same sample. The other is a false standard error: ten blocks of 2,000 points, assumed independent. Both can be measured on a process whose number variance is known exactly, at least for that process. Here that process is GUE in the bulk: the Dumitriu–Edelman tridiagonal model with $N=40{,}000$, keeping the central 20,000 eigenvalues, unfolded by the exact semicircle distribution function. Its number variance is the sine-kernel $\Sigma^2(L)=L-2\int_0^L(L-x)\,\mathrm{sinc}^2x\,dx$, with finite-$N$ corrections far below the effects in question. The estimator is applied exactly as in Part VI, dense windows with step 0.01 and ten blocks, to independent replicate samples.

Correction (Part VIII, §VIII.0) endpoint audit

The quadrature used for every Bogomolny–Keating and GUE prediction in this section started at $x=10^{-4}$ rather than 0, which inflates each prediction by $2La-a^2$ ($1.6\times10^{-3}$ at $L=8$, $3.2\times10^{-3}$ at $L=16$; the local remainder is $O(La^3)$ under the stated near-origin hypothesis). With the origin restored, every residual at $L\ge4$ lies within $\pm0.0007$ and the large-$L$ deficit described here disappears. The deficit is withdrawn; see Part VIII, Table VIII.1.

§VII.4Acquisition: why one adaptive design helps and another hurts

The two programs reached opposite conclusions about adaptive frequency selection, and both are right. The frontier campaign used a common broadband pilot of 33 points followed by eight batches of four queries. Each batch is refitted from observed data only and spends three queries on pole/residue disagreement and one on the largest remaining logarithmic gap. On 200 fresh synthetic systems with known model order, at the same 64 unique queries, it reduced geometric-mean held-out error by 41.7% (ratio 0.583, 95% interval [0.463, 0.725]). Part VI used a 25-point pilot followed by 20 points placed in one shot within two half-bandwidths of the pilot's resonances, with no exploration term. On the two-band Hydrophone plant it increased error at 1% and 5% noise for both fitters. The decisive difference is exploration. Each frontier batch keeps covering the band. Part VI's design concentrates where the pilot already believes the poles are, so a mislocated pilot pole is reinforced rather than corrected, and the slow viscoelastic band is starved. The combined lesson is the frontier's rule: keep a coverage term in every batch and refit between batches. Its own observed-data gating policy, which tried to decide from data when adaptation helps, failed confirmation (ratio 0.976, interval [0.919, 1.063]) and was correctly not promoted.

§VII.5What the merged program now asserts

The two campaigns overlap in four places and agree in all of them. Over $[10,100]$ the zero counts agree tile by tile across independent engines. On identifiability, the frontier campaign's undamped obstruction and Part VII's damped extension together fix the scope of the Part II reconstruction. On passivity, the frontier compiler certifies a restricted RC/RL class with explicit circuit realizations and passes 150 fixtures and 29 adversarial rejections in exact arithmetic. Theorem VI.4.1 certifies any proper rational impedance by Routh–Hurwitz and a Hermite count. The two are complementary: one realizes, the other decides. On fitting, the frontier campaign's 48 protected measured battery cells and the Hydrophone benchmark of Part VI both find vector fitting the strongest unconstrained fitter, with structure and certification adding guarantees rather than accuracy. Both projects publish their negative results: the frontier campaign's failed gating policy and its RC class at 3.48 times vector-fitting error, and Part VI's refuted shell threshold and the 5% regime where the Löwner seed loses.
