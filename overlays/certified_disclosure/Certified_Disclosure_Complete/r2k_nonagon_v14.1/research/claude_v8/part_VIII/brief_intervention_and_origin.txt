The mathematical opening is unusually concrete. Our existing machinery reconstructs networks with full displacement measurements. There is another route: use one fixed force/displacement measurement port, while applying known local changes to grounding stiffness at the hidden nodes. This trades distributed sensing for calibrated intervention access.

Let \(K\) be a connected grounded spring matrix, \(G=K^{-1}\), and define the measured static response

\[
h(\theta)=e_1^{T}
\left(K+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\theta_j e_je_j^{T}\right)^{-1}e_1.
\]

Differentiating gives

\[
\partial_jh(0)=-G_{1j}^{\,2},
\qquad
\partial_i\partial_jh(0)=2G_{1i}G_{ij}G_{1j}.
\]

Because a connected grounded network has \(G_{1j}>0\), these equations recover the entire compliance matrix:

\[
G_{1j}=\sqrt{-\partial_jh(0)},\qquad
G_{ij}=\frac{\partial_i\partial_jh(0)}
{2G_{1i}G_{1j}},
\qquad K=G^{-1}.
\]

One scalar response function therefore contains the whole labeled weighted graph when we can control those local perturbations. Static recovery needs no modal cyclicity, simple eigenvalues or known masses.

More substantially, I worked out a finite replacement for the derivatives:

- One baseline measurement.
- Two grounding strengths at every hidden node.
- One simultaneous grounding experiment for every hidden-node pair.

The total is exactly

\[
1+2(n-1)+\binom{n-1}{2}
=\boxed{\frac{n(n+1)}2}.
\]

That equals the number of independent entries of a symmetric stiffness matrix. The brief supplies the explicit inverse and the physical inequalities selecting its unique solution. I checked the formulas using exact rational arithmetic, including a network with repeated modes.
