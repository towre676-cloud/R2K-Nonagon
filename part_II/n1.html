With Arb's adaptive integrator, a rigorous single-zero count cost 2.2 s at $t=14$ and 14.9 s at $t=1419$, took more than 600 s near the 3000th zero, and had not finished after 100 minutes on the Lehmer tile at $t=7005$. Theorem II.1.7 identifies the cause as ball-wrapping, not arithmetic cost, and removes it. The Taylor-model integrator certifies tiles with a number of segments independent of height, and certifies the Lehmer tile in 19 s. The open remainder is the per-point cost of $\zeta$'s Taylor series, which is $O(t)$ under Euler–Maclaurin. A rigorous Riemann–Siegel Taylor series at $O(\sqrt t)$ per point, or Platt-style batched multi-evaluation shared across the segments of many tiles, would bring tile cost at $t\sim10^{12}$ into the range of production verification. Combined with Corollary II.1.6, one or two zeros per tile, the box criterion would then be a competitive verification method rather than a demonstration.
